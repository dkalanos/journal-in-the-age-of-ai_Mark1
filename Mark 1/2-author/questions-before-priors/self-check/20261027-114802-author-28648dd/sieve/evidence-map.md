## C1 · a gap filled in an existing stream

> Existing models show how theories guide learning, how Bayesian agents discriminate among represented causal structures, and how play can reveal previously unavailable actions. This paper models a different transition: an agent's causal language pools objectively different situations; strategic experience endogenously reveals an action-relevant failure of that pooling and thereby creates a localized open question; costly inquiry activates a fallible technology whose output is not measurable in the agent's prior awareness; only after that output is formulated does the agent construct a prior and undertake Bayesian judgment, under an explicit account of how much confidence the generating experience licenses. Because actions depend on theories, play can either create the contrasts that generate questions or suppress them, so the model also delivers the population-level answer: when theory heterogeneity and performance differences persist, and when the market erases them.

Karni and Vierø (W2145868584, W2115460181) already establish that Bayesian conditioning cannot expand a decision maker's universe and give a rule for beliefs after awareness grows, and Gavetti and Levinthal (W2113812196) and Ehrig and Schmidt (W4206921982) already model coarse or incomplete representations that guide search and can be changed; each overlaps a part of the claim, but none has the change of language triggered endogenously by strategic play exposing a pooling failure, a costly and fallible formulation step preceding prior construction, an account of the confidence that experience licenses, or the population-level persistence result. No candidate anticipates the claimed transition as a whole, so it fills a gap between the growing-awareness stream and the theory-based learning stream; Felin and Zenger (W2062762321) give the informal version of experience-triggered theorizing, and for the population-level part the set offers only background on persistent performance differences from other sources (W2160117959, complexity; W2108767715, differing expectations).
